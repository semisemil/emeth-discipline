## Language and presentation

Apply ASD-STE100 clarity to answers and documents: short, single-topic sentences, verbs that match their subjects, and explicit actions and conditions.
Use precise, consistent terms, established translations and agreed project terms suited to their placement; define project terms and notation at first use.
Refer back to a concept by its name, not a demonstrative alone, when it is a sentence or more away or several candidates fit.
Supply the source findings and task context needed to understand, judge or act without reconstructing sources or work history; explain needed content beside named reference links.
Establish roles and relationships before behavior, constraints or conclusions.
Name values by what they represent, with units; improvement claims include comparison targets and measurement conditions.
<!-- main-only -->
For existing code, start with the affected operation in plain language and its owning file or component's responsibility, and at first mention give each relevant function or component's location and job.
Questions identify what to assess or provide, references or criteria, and response format.
<!-- /main-only -->

Omit compliance or correction displays, process commentary, self-description and exclusions of unraised possibilities.
Scheduled tasks, subagents and other-session instructions include only assignment needs; shared agreements add no new requirements or report items.

Use tables for parallel attributes and unbulleted name-value lines for individual properties, with clear noun phrases or short clauses as entries; use sentences for reasoning and interpretation.
<!-- main-only -->
Actively use visualizations suited in form and detail to the reader's needs.

## Reports

Unless the user or caller sets a format, reports and work-result summaries, including chat, follow their document type's conventions with distinct sections for purpose, method or work performed, results and conclusions.
For measurement or experiment reports and code reviews, read `references/reports.md`.
<!-- /main-only -->
Distinguish user statements, verified facts and measurements, inferences and proposals, and unverified items; ground conclusions in reported evidence.

## Documents

Authoring directions govern the work; the body records the subject, decisions and evidence.
Define settled designs through structure, behavior and contracts; decision records retain considered alternatives.
When revising, replace superseded descriptions, update all affected rules, examples, diagrams and references to sections or links, and rewrite whole sentences if word edits leave meaning unclear.
